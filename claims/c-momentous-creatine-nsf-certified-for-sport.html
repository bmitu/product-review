Momentous Creatine - 90 Servings (Unflavored, 450 g) — Holds up — on a certifier the seller pays

Momentous says: “NSF Certified for Sport®”

their own page · read 2026-09-23

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Momentous says its creatine is NSF Certified for Sport, and the certifier's own register lists it, covering the 450 g tub and all lots. The listing records that testing happened, not the results.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by NSF International (certifier; the listed brand is its customer)

    https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1285010

    The certifier's own listing for this product, read in the plain HTML its server sends, with the brand's full set of 77 listings as the control.

    The certifier's register is the document that decides this claim, and it answers it: the product is listed. Two commands reproduce the finding:

    curl -sL 'https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/search-results.php?brand=Momentous' | grep -c 'results__product-name">Creatine Monohydrate<'
2

curl -sL 'https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1285010' | grep -A3 'Package Size' | sed 's/<[^>]*>//g'
5 g, 75 g, 150 g, 450 g

    The tub on sale is 90 five-gram servings, which is 450 g, and that size is covered. The listing covers all lots, not a named few.

    What the register is and is not matters here. It is published by the certifier, not the brand, so the brand is not vouching for itself. But the certifier describes the companies it lists as its customers: certification is a service the brand buys. That does not make the listing wrong — a certifier whose seal could be bought without passing would have no seal worth buying — but it is why we do not count it as an independent party with no stake in the answer. And a listing is a record that testing happened, not the test results. Nothing in it tells a reader what was measured in which batch.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1756219

    A second listing for the same 450 g tub, read in full, whose label on file still names a creatine source the brand says it no longer uses.

    A reader who searches the register for this exact tub — 450 g, 90 servings — may land on this listing first, and the label shown there is not the label on the tub being sold today. The brand says it changed where its creatine comes from, and this listing still shows the earlier ingredient.

    That is not evidence against the claim. The other listing covers the current label, at 450 g, for every lot. What this shows is how a register goes out of date: a listing tied to named lots of an earlier formula can stay up after the product changes, and the two look the same in the search results. The fix for a reader holding a tub is to read the lot number off the container and check it against the register, which is exactly what the register is built for.

Certification claims are the ones we most often cannot check, for two reasons: the brand names a scheme without pointing to its own entry, or the scheme's public register will not open for anyone checking carefully. Neither happened here. The certifier's search answers an ordinary request and names the product, so this claim can be settled by anyone in a few minutes, and it comes out in the brand's favour.

What that settles is narrow, and a reader should keep it narrow. The claim is that the product holds a particular certification, and it does. The certifier says what its seal is meant to verify: that a product does not contain unsafe levels of contaminants, prohibited substances or masking agents, and that what is on the label matches what is in the product. We did not test any of that, and the register does not publish results. So this record confirms that the certification is real and current. It does not confirm what the certification claims about the powder.

The one wrinkle is worth knowing about if you buy this product. The register holds two listings for what looks like the same tub. One matches the label sold today. The other still shows a label naming a creatine supplier the brand says it has stopped using. Both are live. If the certification is part of why you are buying, read the lot number on your container and look it up — that is the check the register is designed for, and it is the only one that covers the tub in your hand rather than the product in general.

How we checked

  • document — nsfsport.com

    Read on 2026-09-23 from the certifier's public product search, which answers an ordinary web request with no challenge page and no browser scripting needed. Searching by brand returned 77 Momentous listings; two of them are unflavoured Creatine Monohydrate. The one cited above lists the brand as Momentous, country of sale United States, package sizes 5 g, 75 g, 150 g and 450 g, a 5 g serving, and lot numbers 'All'. The label image the certifier keeps on file for it reads 'Creatine Monohydrate (Momentous®)', the same wording as the label on the brand's product page today. The second listing is described in the commentary. We did not test the product, and nothing here is a measurement of what is in the tub.

  • our own desk check — nsfsport.com

    Read on 2026-09-23 from the same public product search. This listing is also unflavoured Creatine Monohydrate from Momentous: 450 g, a 5 g serving, 90 servings, and 24 specific lot numbers rather than 'All'. The label image the certifier keeps for it reads 'Creapure® creatine monohydrate'. The brand's product page, read the same day, answers the question 'Is Momentous Creatine made with Creapure®?' with 'Momentous no longer sources creatine from Creapure®.' The product page's own label reads 'Creatine Monohydrate (Momentous®)'.
