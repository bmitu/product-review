T-fal PTFE nonstick cookware — Does not hold up

T-fal says: “The World Health Organization (WHO), the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), and health authorities in France, Europe (EFSA), and the United States (FDA) have all confirmed that PTFE is harmless to human health.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. T-fal says IARC confirmed PTFE is harmless. IARC put PTFE in Group 3, which by IARC's own definition means it could not classify its cancer risk and is not a finding of safety. We checked the IARC part only.

Evidence

  • document — refutes, independent

    https://monographs.iarc.who.int/list-of-classifications

    IARC's list of classifications, read from the data the page loads: polytetrafluoroethylene is in Group 3, evaluated in 1987.

    IARC evaluates one question only: whether something can cause cancer. It looked at PTFE in a 1979 volume of its Monographs and again in its 1987 update, and placed it in Group 3. That is the group for agents it cannot classify either way. IARC has not said anything about PTFE being harmless, because that is not a question it answers.

  • document — refutes, independent

    https://monographs.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Preamble-2019.pdf

    IARC's Preamble, the document that defines its groups, downloaded and read for the Group 3 definition.

    IARC's own definition of Group 3 is 'not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans', and the Preamble goes on: 'An evaluation in Group 3 is not a determination of non-carcinogenicity or overall safety. It often means that the agent is of unknown carcinogenic potential and that there are significant gaps in research.' That is close to the opposite of the confirmation T-fal describes.

  • our own desk check — refutes, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.t-fal.com/ptfe-nonstick-coating-explained

    T-fal's PTFE explainer page read in full on the same day; it describes the IARC classification twice, both times as an all-clear.

    T-fal cites the right volumes and the right group, then reads the group backwards. Group 3 does not recognise that PTFE is not carcinogenic. It says IARC could not classify it.

This is not a claim about whether PTFE nonstick pans are safe to cook with. It is a claim about what a named authority said, and that can be checked by reading what the authority said.

IARC's job is narrow. It asks whether something can cause cancer and sorts the answer into groups. It has never assessed PTFE's overall safety, because it assesses nothing but cancer. On cancer, it placed PTFE in the group reserved for agents it cannot classify, and it says in plain words that this group is not a finding of safety. T-fal's page cites the right volume and the right group, then presents the group as a clean bill of health.

The same sentence names the WHO, which is IARC's parent body, and food-safety regulators in France, Europe and the United States. We have not checked those. Food regulators do permit PTFE coatings in contact with food, which is a real and relevant fact, but permission to use a material is a regulatory decision, not a finding that it is harmless in every sense. Whether any of them used the word T-fal attributes to them is a separate question, and we have left it open rather than answer it by assumption.
