HexClad HexClad cookware with TerraBond ceramic nonstick (homepage, product pages, FAQ) — Nothing published that a reader could check

HexClad says: “Our cookware features our proprietary TerraBond™ ceramic nonstick coating which is PFAS-free, ensuring a better cooking experience.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. HexClad says its TerraBond ceramic coating is PFAS-free. It does not say what the coating is made of, and the only test behind the claim is a results table from a report it does not publish.

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://hexclad.com/pages/faq

    Every HexClad page we read repeats that TerraBond is PFAS-free; none says what the coating is made of, and none links a test report or a chemical disclosure.

    'Proprietary' is doing a lot of work. A shopper is asked to accept that a coating whose ingredients are secret contains none of a family of thousands of chemicals. The only support offered is a results table from a report HexClad does not publish.

  • document — mixed, independent

    https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.889622/gov.uscourts.cacd.889622.1.0.pdf

    A 2023 class action alleging HexClad sold its earlier pans as free of PFAS and PFOA while they had a PTFE coating. An allegation, not a finding.

    This concerns HexClad's earlier coating, not TerraBond, and HexClad has not admitted it. It is here because it shows that HexClad has made this same kind of 'free from' promise before, about a coating it also did not fully describe, and the promise was challenged in court.

The claim is the kind that could be checked. TerraBond is a ceramic coating, and ceramic nonsticks are generally made without the fluorinated plastics that make up traditional nonstick. HexClad calls the formula proprietary, though, and publishes no ingredient list and no chemical disclosure. The one piece of support is a table of 'Non-Detect' results under a Light Labs logo, which we cover in its own record: no report, no detection limits and no description of what was tested.

The history matters here. In 2023 HexClad was sued for selling its earlier pans as free of PFOA and PFAS while, the complaint says, they had a PTFE coating. That claim was never tested in court. It is a reason to ask for the report rather than take the word.

How we checked

  • our own desk check — hexclad.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 in the HTML the server sends. The claim appears on the homepage and the 10-inch Hybrid pan page in these words. The FAQ says: 'Our cookware products use our proprietary HEXCLAD TerraBond™ ceramic nonstick coating, which is PTFE-free and PFAS-free.' The Science page says the coating is 'PTFE-free and free from forever chemicals'. None of them names an ingredient beyond 'ceramic'. The only test evidence on the site is the unlinked 'Non-Detect' table under a Light Labs logo, which we check in its own record. We found no chemical disclosure page: none is linked from the site, and none exists at the usual addresses for one. Our sweep of HexClad's site (1,344 addresses in its sitemap, 22 pages fetched) found no test report.

  • document — storage.courtlistener.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 from CourtListener's copy of the complaint (C.D. Cal. 2:23-cv-05110, filed 27 June 2023). Paragraph 12 alleges: 'HexClad claims its cookware is "non-toxic" and "free from PFOAs" and PFAS, but the cookware has a PTFE non-stick coating'. It quotes HexClad describing that coating as a 'high-grade non-toxic Japanese coating infused with diamond dust for extra toughness.' The federal case closed on 4 January 2024. A $2.5 million settlement covering pans bought from February 2022 to March 2024 has been reported, but the settlement's own site refused our requests, so we do not rely on its terms.
