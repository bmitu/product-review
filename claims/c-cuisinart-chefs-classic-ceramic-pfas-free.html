Cuisinart Chef's Classic Nonstick Hard Anodized skillets with PURELYCERAMIC interior (12-inch 622C-30H shown) — Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence

Cuisinart says: “PURELYCERAMIC™ nonstick interior. PFAS-Free.”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Cuisinart calls its Chef's Classic ceramic nonstick PFAS-free. Its disclosure for a pan in that line lists nothing fluorinated, and its older nonstick lists three. That holds, but the exact 10- and 12-inch pans are missing from its lookup.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by Cuisinart

    https://www.cuisinart.com/chemical-disclosure

    Cuisinart's disclosure record for the 12-inch skillet with glass cover (622C-30G), same coating and same claim: nine substances, none fluorinated.

    California's cookware law makes a maker list certain chemicals intentionally added to a pan's handle or cooking surface, and PFAS are on that list. Cuisinart's record for a pan from the same ceramic line, sold with the same wording, lists metals and no PFAS.

  • document — supports, funded by Cuisinart

    https://www.cuisinart.com/chemical-disclosure

    For comparison, the record for the older 622-30G, a conventional nonstick pan: it lists PTFE, PFA and FEP.

    This is what gives the ceramic record's silence weight. Where Cuisinart used fluoropolymers, its lookup names all three. The model numbers differ by one letter, and the one with the 'C' lists none.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.cuisinart.com/chemical-disclosure

    The exact 12-inch and 10-inch pans carrying the claim return 'No Records Found' in Cuisinart's lookup; other models, entered the same way, return records.

    The pans a shopper is most likely to buy from this line are not in Cuisinart's own lookup. That is a gap in the paperwork, not evidence of PFAS: the sibling pan with the same coating and the same claim is on file and lists none. A shopper checking the 12-inch or 10-inch pan by its model number will find nothing, and has to know to look up the lidded version instead.

This checks out on Cuisinart's own paperwork, with one gap worth knowing about.

Cuisinart files chemical disclosures model by model. The record for the 12-inch pan with a glass cover, which carries the same 'PURELYCERAMIC' coating and the same 'PFAS-Free' line, lists nine substances and no PFAS. The record for the older conventional nonstick pan, 622-30G, names three fluoropolymers. So Cuisinart does list PFAS where it uses them, and lists none here.

The gap: the 12-inch skillet without a lid and the 10-inch skillet, both sold with the same claim, return 'No Records Found' in the lookup. We checked that the lookup was working by entering other models the same way. A shopper who looks up the pan they are holding will find nothing.

As with every disclosure, this describes what goes into the pan, not a test of what comes out.
