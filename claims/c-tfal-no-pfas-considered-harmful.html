T-fal all T-fal products — Cannot be checked as worded

T-fal says: “T-fal products do not contain PFAS that are considered harmful to health or the environment by health authorities.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

NOT CHECKABLE AS WORDED — no evidence could settle this either way · unfalsifiable as worded

Bottom line. T-fal says its products contain no PFAS that health authorities consider harmful. Its own disclosures say its nonstick pans contain PFAS and are off sale in three states that ban PFAS in cookware. The sentence defines neither term.

The sentence is true or false depending on which authorities count and what 'considered harmful' means, and it names neither. T-fal's own disclosure page says its PTFE-coated pans contain PFAS and that 26 of its cookware ranges have been withdrawn from sale in Minnesota, Maine and Colorado under laws banning cookware with intentionally added PFAS. A reader could take those laws as governments considering PTFE harmful enough to ban from pans. T-fal can answer that they are legislatures, not health authorities, and that the laws ban a whole class of chemicals rather than finding PTFE itself harmful. Both readings are available, the sentence does not choose between them, and no document or measurement can choose for it.

Evidence

  • document — mixed, funded by T-fal (Groupe SEB)

    https://www.t-fal.com/aboutnonstick

    T-fal's own nonstick disclosure page, read in full: the PFAS statement and the list of ranges withdrawn in three states.

    This is T-fal's own page, and it settles the first half of the question plainly: the nonstick pans contain PFAS. What the explainer page adds is that these particular PFAS are not the harmful kind. The disclosure page shows three states have decided, for cookware, not to make that distinction.

  • document — mixed, independent

    https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/116.943

    Minnesota Statutes section 116.943, read from the state's official statute site: the PFAS definition and the cookware ban.

    Minnesota's law does not single out PTFE or find it harmful on its own terms. It treats PFAS as a class, defined broadly enough to include PTFE, and bans the class from cookware. So the law does not directly contradict T-fal's sentence. It does show that a government has declined to draw the line T-fal draws between harmful PFAS and fluoropolymers, at least for pans.

T-fal's argument, set out at length on its explainer page, is that PTFE belongs to the PFAS family by definition but not by behaviour: that the harmful PFAS are the small, mobile ones like PFOA, and that PTFE is an inert plastic. That is a real position held by real scientists, and the sentence above is T-fal's one-line version of it.

The trouble with the one-line version is that it cannot be checked. It does not say which health authorities it means, and 'considered harmful' has no threshold. What can be checked is what sits around it. T-fal's own disclosure says its nonstick pans contain PFAS. The same page lists 26 cookware ranges pulled from sale in Minnesota, Maine and Colorado, which have each banned cookware containing intentionally added PFAS without exempting PTFE. Two pages on the same site, one saying the pans contain nothing the authorities consider harmful and one listing the states where they can no longer be sold, is the useful thing to know. We are not in a position to settle the scientific argument, and the sentence is written so that nobody else can settle it either.

How we checked

  • document — t-fal.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 in the HTML the server sends. It states: 'PTFE coated products contain PFAS chemicals (polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), perfluoroalkoxy alkane (PFA), fluorinated ethylene propylene (FEP)), which are used for their non-stick properties.' It then lists 26 T-fal cookware ranges, plus several kitchen electrics, that 'will not be sold in Minnesota beginning Jan 1, 2025' and, from 1 January 2026, not in Maine or Colorado.

  • document — revisor.mn.gov

    Read on 2026-09-26. Subdivision 1(q) defines PFAS as 'a class of fluorinated organic chemicals containing at least one fully fluorinated carbon atom', which includes PTFE. Subdivision 5(a): 'Beginning January 1, 2025, a person may not sell, offer for sale, or distribute for sale in this state the following products if the product contains intentionally added PFAS', and the list includes cookware.
