Hestan all Hestan cookware (Product Disclosures page) — Cannot be checked as worded

Hestan says: “All Hestan cookware — including our nonstick cookware — is FDA compliant and food safe and not in violation of any federal safety regulations.”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

NOT CHECKABLE AS WORDED — no evidence could settle this either way · unfalsifiable as worded

Bottom line. Hestan says all its cookware is 'FDA compliant and food safe' and breaks no federal safety rule. An FDA rule does allow the PTFE, FEP and PFA it discloses. The rest cannot be checked as worded.

The sentence makes three promises and only one can be checked. 'Food safe' has no legal or technical definition. 'Not in violation of any federal safety regulations' names no regulation, so there is no list to check it against. 'FDA compliant' can be partly checked: the materials Hestan discloses for its nonstick are ones an FDA regulation allows in contact with food. But the FDA does not inspect or certify pans, and whether a given coating meets that regulation's limits on what can leach out would take a lab test Hestan does not publish.

Evidence

  • document — mixed, funded by Hestan

    https://hestanculinary.com/pages/product-disclosures

    Hestan's disclosures page, read in full: it lists PTFE, FEP and PFA for its nonstick and cites 21 CFR in general, but no specific rule or test.

    The page is candid about what the nonstick is made of, which is what makes the FDA half partly checkable. It cites the whole of Title 21 rather than any rule in it, and offers nothing a reader could use to check 'food safe' or 'any federal safety regulations'.

  • document — mixed, independent

    https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-177/subpart-B/section-177.1550

    The FDA rule on perfluorocarbon resins, read from the official eCFR: it covers PTFE, FEP and PFA, subject to limits on what can be extracted from them.

    This is the part of 'FDA compliant' that holds: the three fluoropolymers Hestan names are the ones this rule allows in food-contact coatings. What it cannot show is that Hestan's coating meets the rule's extraction limits. That takes a test, and the FDA does not run one on any pan before it is sold.

This sentence sits on Hestan's chemical disclosures page, directly after Hestan says its nonstick contains PTFE, FEP and PFA, all PFAS. Read in that place, it is an answer to a worried shopper: yes, there is PFAS, and it is allowed.

The allowed part is true as far as it goes. An FDA rule permits those three materials in coatings that touch food. But the FDA does not approve or test pans, so 'FDA compliant' means Hestan believes its coating meets the rule, not that anyone has checked. 'Food safe' has no definition, and 'not in violation of any federal safety regulations' names none. A shopper deciding whether to accept PTFE learns what the coating is from this page, which is worth something. They do not learn whether it is safe from this sentence.

How we checked

  • document — hestanculinary.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in the HTML the server sends. The claim sentence follows a paragraph saying 'PTFE, PFA and FEP are all fluoropolymers and are classified as PFAS substances. They are used in food contact coating because of their superior nonstick properties.' The table for 'Hestan Culinary Nonstick Cookware' lists aluminum, copper, iron, manganese, chromium, nickel, phosphorus and 'Polytetrafluoroethylene, fluorinated ethylene and propylene, and perfluoroalkoxy alkane polymer'. Later: 'All food-contact materials used in our products comply with applicable U.S. Food and Drug Administration regulations under Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR) governing substances in contact with food', and 'adherence to FDA frameworks for food-contact materials (21 CFR/FCN program)'. No section number, test, laboratory or result is given.

  • document — ecfr.gov

    Read on 2026-09-27 from the eCFR's rendering service for 21 CFR 177.1550, 'Perfluorocarbon resins'. It opens: 'Perfluorocarbon resins identified in this section may be safely used as articles or components of articles intended to contact food, subject to the provisions of this section'. Paragraph (a) covers resins made from tetrafluoroethylene and hexafluoropropylene (PTFE and FEP) and from perfluoropropylvinylether with tetrafluoroethylene (PFA), and says 'The resins shall meet the extractives limitations in paragraph (d) of this section.'
