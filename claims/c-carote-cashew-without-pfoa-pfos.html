Carote Cashew Detachable Cookware Set, 19 pieces — Cannot be checked as worded

Carote says: “Cook with confidence using Carote’s nonstick cookware, made without PFOA, PFOS, or other harmful chemicals—designed for safer, easier everyday meals.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

NOT CHECKABLE AS WORDED — no evidence could settle this either way · unfalsifiable as worded

Bottom line. Carote says the Cashew set is made without PFOA, PFOS 'or other harmful chemicals'. It never says what the coating is. Its ceramic Sakura set rules out PTFE and PFAS by name; this page does not.

The promise that carries the sentence is 'or other harmful chemicals', and it names neither the chemicals nor what counts as harmful. Any substance a reader worries about can be answered with 'we do not consider that harmful'. The two chemicals it does name, PFOA and PFOS, could be checked by a lab test of the pan, but Carote publishes none, and the page does not say what the coating is made of, so there is not even an ingredient list to read against the claim.

    This is the useful comparison. On one product page Carote rules out PTFE and PFAS by name. On the Cashew page it rules out only PFOA and PFOS. That does not prove the Cashew coating contains PTFE; it means Carote has chosen not to say, on a site where it does say it elsewhere.

The sentence names two chemicals, then leaves 'other harmful chemicals' for the reader to fill in. The page calls the same coating titanium, hybrid and ceramic in different places and never gives its chemistry. We found no test report and no chemical disclosure anywhere on Carote's site.

The telling part is on Carote's own site. Its Sakura ceramic set says 'no PTFE, PFOA, PFAS, lead, or cadmium'. The Cashew page stops at PFOA and PFOS. We cannot tell from the outside whether the Cashew coating contains PTFE, and we are not saying it does. We are saying that Carote knows how to rule PTFE out when it can, and here it has not. A shopper avoiding PTFE or PFAS should read the Cashew page as silent on both.
