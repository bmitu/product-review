All-Clad Ceramic Nonstick cookware (NS Pro and HA1 Ceramic Nonstick) — Does not hold up — on evidence from an interested party

All-Clad says: “Each Ceramic Nonstick piece is backed by All-Clad’s limited lifetime warranty, reflecting our commitment to quality materials, thoughtful design, and cookware you can trust in your kitchen.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. All-Clad's warranty page says its ceramic nonstick has a lifetime warranty. The warranty document linked beneath that sentence says one year, and it excludes coating wear. The one-year figure on the product page is the right one.

Evidence

  • document — refutes, funded by All-Clad (All-Clad Metalcrafters, LLC; Groupe SEB)

    https://images.salsify.com/image/upload/s--d8bvWi8v--/enrfvibwlosvriylgt6i

    All-Clad's own two-page ceramic cookware warranty, downloaded from the link under the claim and read in full.

    The document is titled 'All-Clad Ceramic Coated Cookware — One-Year Warranty', and its operative sentence says the cookware 'will be free from defects in material, construction, or workmanship under normal use and following care instructions for one year.' Its exclusions include normal wear, scratches, damage from scouring pads, dishwasher cleaning, and ceramic coating deterioration from high or prolonged heat. So the warranty is a year long and leaves out coating wear, which is how ceramic nonstick usually wears out.

    This is All-Clad's own document, so nobody outside the company is being quoted against it. It does not need anyone else, because it is the warranty itself. On a question about what a warranty says, the warranty is the only authority, and it is the one All-Clad chose to link.

  • our own desk check — refutes, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.all-clad.com/ns-pro-ceramic-nonstick-fry-pan-with-lid-10-inch.html

    Both All-Clad pages read in full on the same day, plus the general lifetime warranty document, to compare what each covers.

    All-Clad's own site gives two answers about the same pans. The product page, where people buy, says one year, and so does the warranty document. The warranty page, where someone goes to check before they buy, says lifetime. Two of the three agree, and one of those two is the contract.

    It is easy to see how the mistake happened. The paragraph above it on the same page covers All-Clad's cookware, knives, accessories and bakeware in general, which carry a limited lifetime warranty, and the ceramic sentence repeats that paragraph's phrasing. That general lifetime document does say it covers 'any All-Clad cookware', and a determined reader could argue that ceramic pans are cookware. But All-Clad publishes a separate warranty written specifically for its ceramic coated cookware, files it under the ceramic heading, and links that one, not the lifetime one, from the ceramic pan's own product page. The specific document is the one that governs, and it says one year.

This is a small claim with a large price attached. A reader deciding between a ceramic pan and a stainless one is often weighing exactly this: ceramic coatings wear out, and a lifetime warranty would take that risk off the buyer. All-Clad's warranty page says it does. The contract that page links to says one year, and it does not cover the coating wearing down, being scratched, or deteriorating from heat.

We are not suggesting anyone was misled on purpose. The product page gets it right, and the wrong sentence reads like a copy of the general cookware paragraph above it. But a warranty page is where careful shoppers go to settle this question, and on the day we read it, it gave the wrong answer about the product most likely to need a warranty.

If All-Clad corrects the page, this record should be read again. The finding is about what the page said on the date above, and about which document governs, which is the contract.
