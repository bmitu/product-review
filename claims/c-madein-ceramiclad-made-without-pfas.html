Made In CeramiClad 2.0 Non Stick Frying Pan (10-inch page) — Nothing published that a reader could check

Made In says: “All CeramiClad™ pieces are made without: PFAS PFOA PTFE Lead Cadmium BPA Third-Party Tested For Safety:”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Made In says CeramiClad is made without PFAS, PTFE, PFOA, lead, cadmium and BPA, 'third-party tested'. Its lab report checks only 30 PFAS, and predates the current '2.0' coating by seven months.

Evidence

  • measurement — mixed, funded by Made In

    https://app.lightlabs.com/comprehensive-testing-report/69/244

    Light Labs' CeramiClad report: 30 PFAS, each below a limit per square inch of surface. It has no PTFE, lead, cadmium or BPA result.

    The PFAS results are real and specific, and reported per area of surface, which suits a coated pan. But the 30 compounds are small PFAS molecules. PTFE, the plastic in traditional nonstick, is not one of the 30, and a test for small molecules is not built to find a large polymer, and PTFE is the thing a ceramic-pan buyer is usually trying to avoid. Lead, cadmium and BPA are not tested at all.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://madeincookware.com/products/ceramic-frying-pan/10-inch

    The page sells 'CeramiClad 2.0' with 'a completely reimagined ceramic coating', lists six things it is made without, then shows the lab panel.

    Laid out this way, a reader would take all six items as tested. The report covers one of them. The page also does not say which coating the report tested. Made In announced the '2.0' coating in April 2026, seven months after the test (see the launch post below).

  • document — mixed, funded by Made In

    https://madeincookware.com/blogs/introducing-ceramiclad-2-0-made-in-ceramic-pans

    Made In's CeramiClad 2.0 launch post is dated 15 April 2026, seven months after the lab test. Archived product pages first say '2.0' in spring 2026.

    The lab report shown on the 2.0 page was 'last tested' on 22 September 2025. That is seven months before Made In announced the new coating, and the product page still sold the original then. Unless Made In sent the lab early samples of the new coating, which the report does not say, the test is of the coating Made In says it rebuilt. The post also says 2.0 was tested for lead, cadmium and arsenic, but none of those results are published.

Made In publishes a readable lab report for its ceramic pans, one click from the product page, and that is more than most ceramic brands on this page do. It finds none of 30 PFAS chemicals above small stated limits.

The trouble is what the page stacks on top of it. It lists six things the coating is made without, then 'Third-Party Tested For Safety', and the report covers only the PFAS. The question most people buying a ceramic pan are asking is whether it has PTFE, and a test for small PFAS molecules is not built to answer that. Lead and cadmium, which matter for ceramic glazes, are not tested either. The product page does not name the coating; Made In's launch post calls it 'silica-alumina'. And the report was run in September 2025, seven months before Made In announced '2.0' with a rebuilt coating, so it most likely tested the old one. So the absence claims may well be true, but for five of the six, Made In's word is the only evidence.

How we checked

  • measurement — app.lightlabs.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 from the lab's own domain, in the HTML its server sends. Product 'CeramiClad®'. One group, PFAS, 'Last tested Sep 22, 2025': 30 compounds, counted one by one, each reported below a limit of 0.005, 0.01 or 0.09 nanograms per square inch, including PFOA (< 0.01) and PFOS (< 0.005). Each carries the note 'Substance within limit set by Made In Cookware'. There is no bisphenol, metals or fluoropolymer group. The report says: 'Results reflect laboratory analysis of submitted samples at the time of testing as reported by the testing laboratory.' It does not describe the sample or name a method. The page's Light Labs panel is chosen by the page's address; 'ceramic-frying-pan' is one of this report's matching addresses in Light Labs' own product feed for Made In.

  • our own desk check — madeincookware.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in the HTML the server sends. The product is titled 'CeramiClad® 2.0 Non Stick Frying Pan' ($149, 10 inch), 'Made in Italy', with 'a completely reimagined ceramic coating' that is '43% more durable than the original CeramiClad®'. It says 'Made without PFAS, PTFE, PFOA, lead, or cadmium' and 'Our super-durable proprietary coating is free of PFAS, PTFE, PFOA, lead, and cadmium', and under 'Safety & Health' lists PFAS, PFOA, PTFE, lead, cadmium and BPA followed by 'Third-Party Tested For Safety:' and the Light Labs panel. The coating's chemistry is not named. Made In's California disclosure page lists PTFE, FEP and PFA for its nonstick line and does not mention CeramiClad.

  • document — madeincookware.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 on Made In's own site, in the HTML its server sends; the page's metadata gives 'datePublished' 2026-04-15. The post says: 'We rebuilt our ceramic non-stick coating from the surface down. Not a new color, not a new handle, not a marketing refresh — a new coating architecture with a reinforced bond layer that didn't exist in the original CeramiClad®.' It names the chemistry: 'CeramiClad® 2.0 uses silica-alumina chemistry. The same clean foundation as the original.' It says 'we have the extraction data to back it' and ends 'no PFAS, PFOA, lead, cadmium, or arsenic detected', but links no report, lab or result for lead, cadmium or arsenic. The same text is in the Internet Archive's capture of 19 May 2026, its only capture of the post. To check the date, we read the Internet Archive's captures of the product page: the ones of 28 October 2025 and 10 March 2026 never mention '2.0'; the one of 15 May 2026 mentions it 86 times.
