Our Place Always Pan 2.0 (ceramic nonstick) — Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence

Our Place says: “Thermakind® ceramic nonstick coating made without PFAS”

their own page · read 2026-09-23

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Our Place's lab report on its ceramic coating finds none of the 689 PFAS it names and no total fluorine. Our Place paid for it, and it gives no detection limits and no description of the sample.

Evidence

  • measurement — supports, funded by Our Place

    https://app.lightlabs.com/comprehensive-testing-report/628/3916

    The lab's report on Our Place's ceramic coating, read from the lab's own site: 692 results, every one not detected, all counted.

    This is a real report from a named lab, and every one of its 692 results comes back not detected: PFOA, 688 other PFAS compounds, a total-fluorine screen, lead and cadmium. For a claim that the coating is made without PFAS, that is the right kind of evidence.

    It has limits a reader should see. It never says how low 'not detected' goes: the page defines it only as 'below detectable levels as defined by third-party lab tests', with no number for any result. It names no test method. It says what was tested only as a coating sample, not whether it was a finished pan, which production run, or new or used, and its own footer calls the results an analysis of 'submitted samples at the time of testing'. It carries an 'ISO 17025 Accredited Lab' badge but names no accrediting body or certificate. And Our Place paid for it; the page says Our Place 'partners with' the lab. None of that makes the result wrong. It makes it a result we can read but not check.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://fromourplace.com/pages/third-party-testing

    Our read of the brand's testing page: it loads a different product's report first, and the ceramic results are one menu choice away.

    Two things we found while checking, neither of which changes the verdict.

    First, the brand's testing page opens on the wrong pan for this claim. It shows the titanium range's report by default, and a reader has to pick the ceramic line from a menu to see the results that bear on the Always Pan. The results turn out to be the same, but a reader should look at the right one.

    Second, PTFE, the material Teflon is made from and the one most people mean when they worry about nonstick pans, is not among the report's named results. The product page counts PTFE as a PFAS and says the coating is made without it. The report's total-fluorine result is not detected, and the lab describes that test as one that 'screens for both known and unknown compounds that targeted tests may miss'. But with no method stated, we cannot tell you whether that screen would have picked up PTFE in this sample. So the report supports 'made without PFAS' as a whole. It does not separately confirm the PTFE part by name.

'Made without PFAS' is a claim about what a coating is made of, and the usual problem with it is that nothing is offered to back it. Our Place offers something: a lab report, published on the lab's own site, reached from the brand's testing page. We read it end to end. Every one of its 692 results is not detected.

That is enough to call the claim supported, and not enough to go further. The report is Our Place's own, paid for by the company, and no one outside it has tested the pan. It also leaves out the things that would let a reader weigh a 'not detected': how low the test could see, what method was used, and what exactly went to the lab. A result of 'none found' from a test that can only see large amounts means much less than the same result from one that sees traces, and this report does not say which it is.

For a buyer, the fair reading is this. The brand has put a specific test result in public, where anyone can read it. It is favourable, and it is the brand's own. If PTFE in particular is your concern, note that the report does not name it, though its total-fluorine screen came back empty.

This claim also sits next to a louder one. The same page sells the pan as 'non-toxic'. That word promises something about safety in use, and no report of this kind can settle it. We have not rated it here.

How we checked

  • measurement — app.lightlabs.com

    Retrieved 2026-09-23 from the lab's own domain, in the HTML its server sends. The report is titled for Our Place's Ceramic Nonstick Cookware, sample 'Ceramic Nonstick Coating', every group dated 6 May 2026. It holds four groups: heavy metals (cadmium, lead), total organic fluorine, PFAS (PFOA) and other PFAS (688 compounds). As a completeness check we counted result lines group by group: 2 + 1 + 1 + 688 = 692, each one 'Not detected', and none with a number attached. The page's own pager agrees: 688 compounds shown five to a page is the 138 pages it says it has. The lab's four explanatory 'Not detected means…' sentences are excluded from that count.

  • our own desk check — fromourplace.com

    On 2026-09-23 the brand's testing page contained a lab widget set to the report for Our Place's Titanium Pro Cookware (sample 'Titanium NoCo Cookware Technology', tested 6 April 2026), not the ceramic coating. The widget's own product menu switches to the ceramic report cited above, at a separate address on the lab's site. Compared line by line, the two reports list the same compounds with the same result for every one; only the product, sample and test date differ. The product page carrying the claim also names PTFE: its FAQ says the coating is made without 'PFAS (including PTFEs and PFOAs)'. We searched the ceramic report's results for 'PTFE', 'polytetrafluoro' and 'Teflon' and found none.
