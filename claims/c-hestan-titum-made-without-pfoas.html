Hestan TITUM Fusion Nonstick Skillets — Nobody has measured this

Hestan says: “TITUM Nonstick interior and exterior made without PFOA's”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable only by a new independent measurement

Bottom line. Hestan says its TITUM nonstick is made without PFOA, and says plainly on the same page that the coating is PTFE. It publishes no test, and its own article defines the claim as 'no intentionally added PFOA'.

What would settle it: A targeted PFAS panel on the nonstick interior and exterior of new production TITUM Fusion pans, including PFOA, reported with its detection limit.
Estimated cost: not yet priced

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://hestanculinary.com/products/nonstick-skillets

    The product page says 'made without PFOA's' and, on the same page, that the nonstick finish is PTFE-based. It links no test.

    Unlike most nonstick product pages we have read, this one says what the coating is. A shopper who wants to avoid PTFE has the answer on the page. The PFOA line is the part nobody can check from the outside.

  • document — mixed, funded by Hestan

    https://hestanculinary.com/blogs/news/are-non-toxic-cookware-sets-also-high-performance

    Hestan's own article defines the claim as 'No intentionally added PFOA' and mentions 'third-party evaluation' without naming a lab or a result.

    This is Hestan saying what it means: PFOA is not an ingredient. That is a statement about the recipe, not a measurement of the pan, and it is a narrower promise than 'made without' may sound.

  • document — mixed, independent

    https://www.epa.gov/assessing-and-managing-chemicals-under-tsca/risk-management-and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas

    EPA says eight leading PFAS makers committed in 2006 to work toward eliminating PFOA from their products by 2015, and that all met the program's goals.

    This is context, not a check of Hestan's pan. It is part of why 'PFOA-free' says little about a nonstick pan bought today: the largest makers of these chemicals committed a decade ago to stop putting PFOA in their products. We do not know who supplies Hestan's coating, so we cannot say this program covers it.

Hestan does something most nonstick brands here do not: it names the coating. The TITUM Fusion page says the finish is PTFE, which is a PFAS, so nobody reading the whole page is misled about that.

The 'made without PFOA's' line is harder to weigh. Only a test of the pan could confirm it, and Hestan publishes none. Its own article says what the promise means: PFOA is not an ingredient in the recipe, which is not the same as a measured absence. It also says little. The largest makers of these chemicals committed with the US regulator to work PFOA out of their products by 2015, and the regulator says all of them met the program's goals. So the line may well be true, and it tells a shopper little that the PTFE disclosure next to it does not.

How we checked

  • our own desk check — hestanculinary.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in the HTML the server sends. Under 'Details You'll Love': 'TITUM Nonstick interior and exterior made without PFOA's'. The page's feature strip says 'FDA-Approved PTFE Nonstick', and a paragraph headed 'Disclosure' reads: 'Hestan TITUM® Fusion features a PTFE-based nonstick finish, engineered for outstanding durability and long-lasting performance. Unlike some alternative nonstick materials, PTFE is authorized for food contact under U.S. FDA (21 CFR) and European Union (EFSA) regulations'. The pans are described as handcrafted in Italy. Our sweep of Hestan's site (1,905 addresses in its sitemap, 31 pages fetched) found no test report and named no laboratory.

  • document — hestanculinary.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in the HTML the server sends. It reads: 'No intentionally added PFOA: Hestan TITUM Nonstick and other premium nonstick products are not engineered using PFOA. Hestan TITUM® Nonstick features a PTFE-based nonstick finish'. Later: 'we practice strict safety, migration testing and third-party evaluation of our materials'. No laboratory, report, method or result is named or linked. A web-search summary described Hestan's nonstick as 'found to be compliant by independent 3rd party laboratory testing'; that sentence is not on this article, the product page or the TITUM story pages, so we do not rely on it.

  • document — epa.gov

    Read on 2026-09-27. Under 'PFOA Stewardship Program': 'In 2006, EPA invited eight major leading companies in the per- and PFAS industry to join in a global stewardship program' with the goals of a 95 percent cut in PFOA 'product content levels' by 2010 and 'working toward the elimination of these chemicals from emissions and products by 2015.' Participants named: Arkema, Asahi, BASF, Clariant, Daikin, 3M/Dyneon, DuPont, Solvay Solexis. 'All companies have met the PFOA Stewardship Program goals.' The page does not name Hestan or its coating supplier, and the program covered only those eight companies.
