Lodge Seasoned cast iron (Classic Cast Iron Skillet and the seasoned line) — Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence

Lodge says: “Just like all Lodge Cast Iron, this skillet is nontoxic, naturally seasoned, and made without PFAS”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Lodge says its cast iron is made without PFAS. Its disclosure lists every material in the seasoned line, iron, trace elements and soybean oil, with nothing fluorinated. That holds; 'nontoxic', in the same sentence, is not a checkable claim.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by Lodge Cast Iron

    https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/california-disclosure

    Lodge's California disclosure page read in full, including its chart of every material in every product type.

    Lodge's disclosure goes further than the law asks. California requires a list of certain chemicals intentionally added to handles and cooking surfaces, and Lodge instead lists everything in each product, marking the ingredients that are not on the state's list. For seasoned cast iron that is iron, a handful of elements found in iron and steel, and soybean oil. Nothing fluorinated appears anywhere in the chart, for any product.

  • our own desk check — supports, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.lodgecastiron.com/products/round-cast-iron-classic-skillet

    The Classic Cast Iron Skillet page read in full; it repeats the claim in its feature list and links the disclosure.

    Seasoned cast iron is about as simple as a pan gets: cast iron and baked-on oil. Lodge links its full ingredient chart from the product page itself, which is the right place for it.

This checks out, and Lodge makes it easy to check. Its California disclosure lists every material in each product line, not just the chemicals the law asks about, and the seasoned cast iron is iron and soybean oil. There is no coating, and nothing in the chart could be PFAS.

Two limits. First, a disclosure describes what goes into the pan, and nobody has published a test of what comes out of one. For a bare iron pan there is no obvious route for PFAS to get in. Second, the same sentence calls the skillet 'nontoxic', a word with no definition and no threshold. We have not rated that part. The PFAS part is the claim a shopper can use, and it holds.

The page says 'Our cookware is made without PFOA and PTFE' and then gives a chart of every material in each product type, including ones California does not list. Seasoned cast iron (Classic, Bakeware, Blacklock, Chef Collection, Lodge BOLD, Grilling & Outdoors): iron, phosphorus, manganese and manganese compounds, strontium, carbon, silicon, sulfur, steel, and soybean-oil seasoning. Seasoned carbon steel: steel and soybean-oil seasoning. Enameled cast iron adds antimony compounds, steel, nickel and nickel compounds, copper and chromium compounds, and, for 'Red and Orange items only', cadmium compounds and sulfate. Glass lids: manganese.
