HexClad HexClad Hybrid cookware with TerraBond ceramic nonstick (10-inch Hybrid pan page) — Nothing published that a reader could check

HexClad says: “Test Results Verified by 3rd Party Lab Tested our pots and pans for PFAS compounds and all results were below the laboratory's detection limits, known as "Non-Detect".”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. HexClad says a third-party lab found no PFAS in its pots and pans, and shows a table of 'Non-Detect' results under a Light Labs logo. It publishes no report, no detection limits and no description of the sample.

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://hexclad.com/products/10-hexclad-pan

    HexClad's product page shows a results table under a Light Labs logo, but links no report, and gives no detection limit, sample or date.

    This is more than most brands show: named compounds and a named lab. It is still HexClad's summary of a test, not the test. Without the report a reader cannot see the detection limits, which decide what 'Non-Detect' means, and cannot see which pan or coating was sampled, or when.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.lightlabs.com/anti-certification

    Light Labs publishes client reports on its own domain, and did so for Our Place; we found none there for HexClad.

    This is not proof that no report exists. Light Labs' reports sit at addresses nobody can list, so a report could exist without us finding it. It does mean HexClad has not published one: Light Labs' tool for doing so is not on HexClad's page, and HexClad links no report anywhere we could reach.

HexClad names its lab, which is Light Labs, the same lab behind the Our Place report we could read in full. And it shows results for specific compounds rather than a bare 'PFAS-free'. The difference from Our Place is that Our Place's report can be opened and HexClad's cannot. The table is HexClad's own page element, not the lab's report, and it links nowhere.

That matters because 'Non-Detect' only means 'below the limit this method could see', and the limit is exactly what the table leaves out. The table also lists 'PTFE' and 'PFAS' as single results. PTFE is a plastic that standard PFAS tests do not measure directly, and 'PFAS' is a family of thousands of chemicals. A report would say how each was tested. The claim may be true, and a reader who wants to check it has nothing to open.

How we checked

  • our own desk check — hexclad.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 in the HTML the server sends. Under the claim sits a table of eight rows, each marked 'Passed' and 'Non-Detect': PFOS; PTFE; PFOA; PFAS; PFPeS; PFBS; 'PFPeA, PFHpS, PFDA, 9Cl-PF3ONS, 11Cl-PF3OUdS'; PFNA. Below it: 'Any potential presence is so low it can't even be detected. We're talking less than a drop in an Olympic-sized pool.' The only sign of the lab is a Light Labs logo image served from HexClad's own theme files. The table is part of HexClad's page, not an embedded Light Labs report, and it contains no links. Our sweep of HexClad's site (1,347 addresses in its sitemap, 22 pages fetched) found the same logo on two other pages and no test report.

  • our own desk check — lightlabs.com

    Read on 2026-09-26. Light Labs sells brands a way to 'Embed real lab results' on their sites, and the Our Place report we cite elsewhere is served from app.lightlabs.com. That domain has no public index: its sitemap and brand paths return 404, and a web search restricted to it found no HexClad page. The data feed behind Light Labs' embeddable panel answers by company number, so we asked it for every number from 1 to 1,615. 216 companies answered, with 1,112 products between them, and none of those products matches a HexClad page; 1,189 companies' feeds refused outside requests, and we did not try to get around that. Light Labs' marketing site (75 addresses in its sitemap) lists no brands or reports. A general web search found third-party write-ups repeating HexClad's 'Non-Detect' table, and one saying outright that HexClad does not publish the report; none linked to one.
