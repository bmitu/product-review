Caraway Ceramic Cookware Set — Cannot be checked as worded

Caraway says: “Non-Toxic Coating”

their own page · read 2026-09-20

NOT CHECKABLE AS WORDED — no evidence could settle this either way · unfalsifiable as worded

Bottom line. “Non-toxic” is not a claim any test can check. The specific absences Caraway lists underneath it are, and each one can be checked on its own.

'Non-toxic' names no substance, no exposure route, no dose and no threshold, and no US regulator defines the term for cookware. There is no measurement whose result could contradict it. Caraway's own site does not define it either: the testing page carries the heading 'What does "non-toxic" mean for Caraway cookware and bakeware?' and the materials page drops the word entirely, substituting a list of absences — 'It's made without PTFE, PFOA, PFAS, lead, and cadmium, and third-party tested for over 600 types of forever chemicals.' Those absences are type-A claims and each is separately checkable. 'Non-toxic' is the conclusion stacked on top of them, and it does not follow: a coating can be free of five named substances, and of 600 tested ones, and still contain the 601st that nobody assayed.

Evidence

  • None is possible. The claim names nothing a measurement could contradict.

The point of this record is not that Caraway is being dishonest. The brand tests more than most of its competitors, and the absences it lists may well all hold. The point is narrower: those absences are checkable and we will check them, while the word resting on top of them is not, and no amount of testing underneath it ever makes it so.

Worth publishing because “non-toxic” is the phrase a shopper actually types into a search box. It is the part of the label doing the selling, and it is the one part that cannot be wrong — because it cannot be right either.
