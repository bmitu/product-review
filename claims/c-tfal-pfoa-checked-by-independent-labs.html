T-fal all T-fal nonstick cookware (FAQ on the Professional VX3 Platinum 12-inch fry pan page) — Nothing published that a reader could check

T-fal says: “NO. Cookware items with T-fal non-stick coating do not contain PFOA. This is regularly checked by independent laboratories, which verify the absence of PFOA in the finished products. Since 2003, tests have regularly been performed by these independent laboratories in many countries (Aromalyse and Ianesco in France, TüvSud in Hong Kong and SGS in China). These tests have systematically proven the absence of PFOA in products with T-fal non-stick coating.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. T-fal says independent labs have confirmed since 2003 that its nonstick pans contain no PFOA, and names four of them. It publishes none of their reports. Its own disclosure says the coating is made of other PFAS.

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.t-fal.com/professional-vx3-platinum-non-stick-12-inch-frypan.html

    The product page and T-fal's two nonstick pages read in full; four labs are named and no report from any of them is linked.

    Naming four laboratories is more than Lodge or GreenPan offer for their own lab claims. It is still a list of names, not results: no report, no date, no product, no detection limit. 'Absence' in a lab test means 'below what the method could detect', and without the reports a reader cannot know what that limit was.

  • document — mixed, funded by T-fal (Groupe SEB)

    https://www.t-fal.com/aboutnonstick

    T-fal's own nonstick disclosure page, read in full: its nonstick pans contain PTFE, PFA and FEP, all of them PFAS.

    This does not bear on PFOA either way. It is here because it is the other half of the picture: 'no PFOA' is a statement about one chemical, and T-fal's own disclosure says the coating is made of others in the same family.

T-fal goes further than most. It names four laboratories and says they have tested its pans for PFOA since 2003. We looked for the results and found none: not on the product page, the PTFE explainer or the disclosure page, and not anywhere else on T-fal's site that our sweep reached. So the claim rests on T-fal's word that the tests exist and came back clean.

The claim may well be true, and it is also narrower than it sounds. T-fal's explainer says it and its suppliers have not used PFOA in their processes since 2012, which is to say they did before then. T-fal's own disclosure says the coating itself is PTFE, PFA and FEP, which are all PFAS. A shopper trying to avoid PFAS will not get there from this claim, and a shopper who wants to see the PFOA tests cannot.
