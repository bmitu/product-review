Thorne Creatine - 90 Servings (Unflavored, 450 g) — Holds up — certified by a third-party lab (NSF International)

Thorne says: “NSF Certified for Sport®”

their own page · read 2026-09-23

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Thorne says its creatine is NSF Certified for Sport, and the certifier's register lists the 450 g tub for 65 named lots. Check your lot number. The listing records that testing happened, not the results.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by NSF International (certifier; the listed brand is its customer)

    https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1495542

    The certifier's own listing for this tub, read in the plain HTML its server sends; it covers the 450 g size for 65 named lots, not for every lot.

    The certifier's register is the document that decides this claim, and it answers it: the tub is listed. Three commands reproduce the finding:

    curl -sL 'https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1495542' | tr '\n' ' ' | sed 's/<[^>]*>/ /g' | tr -s ' ' | grep -oE 'Package Size [0-9][^A-Z]*'
Package Size 450 g, 900 g

curl -sL 'https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/listing-detail.php?id=1495542' | tr '\n' ' ' | sed 's/<[^>]*>/ /g' | grep -oE 'Lot # .* Product Form' | grep -oE '\b[0-9]{6}\b' | wc -l
65

    The tub on sale is 90 five-gram servings, which is 450 g, and that size is covered. Unlike some listings in the same register, this one does not cover 'All' lots. It covers 65 named ones. A tub whose lot number is on the list is covered by this certification; a tub whose lot is not on the list may be newer than the last update, or may not have been certified, and the register cannot tell you which.

    What the register is and is not matters here. It is published by the certifier, not the brand, so the brand is not vouching for itself. But the certifier describes the companies it lists as its customers: certification is a service the brand buys. That does not make the listing wrong — a certifier whose seal could be bought without passing would have no seal worth buying — but it is why we do not count it as an independent party with no stake in the answer. And a listing is a record that testing happened, not the test results. Nothing in it tells a reader what was measured in which batch.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.nsfsport.com/certified-products/search-results.php?brand=Thorne%C2%AE

    The register files this brand under two spellings, and the one with the ® mark does not include the listing above; it shows an older 453 g listing instead.

    A reader who picks the brand name with the ® mark — the way it is printed on the product — finds a creatine powder listing, but not the one that covers the tub on sale now. The 453 g listing covers only 7 lots, and its label differs from today's by one word. Someone checking a lot number against that listing alone would probably not find their lot and could conclude the tub is not certified.

    That is not evidence against the claim. The listing that matches today's tub exists and is live; it is filed under the other spelling. What this shows is that a register can be correct and still be easy to misread. If you are checking a tub, search both spellings, or search by product type rather than brand, before deciding your lot is missing.

This is the second creatine certification we have checked against the same register, and it comes out the same way: the product is listed, and anyone can confirm that in a few minutes. The certifier's search answers an ordinary request and names the product.

What that settles is narrow, and a reader should keep it narrow. The claim is that the product holds a particular certification, and it does. The certifier says what its seal is meant to verify: that a product does not contain unsafe levels of contaminants, prohibited substances or masking agents, and that what is on the label matches what is in the product. We did not test any of that, and the register does not publish results. So this record confirms that the certification is real and current. It does not confirm what the certification claims about the powder.

Two details matter if the certification is part of why you are buying. First, the listing covers named lots, not every lot, so the lot number printed on your tub is what decides whether that tub is covered. Second, the register files this brand under two spellings, with and without the ® mark, and the listing for today's tub is under the plain one. Search both before concluding that your lot is not there.

How we checked

  • document — nsfsport.com

    Read on 2026-09-23 from the certifier's public product search, which answers an ordinary web request with no challenge page and no browser scripting needed. The listing cited above names the brand as Thorne, country of sale United States, unflavoured powder, package sizes 450 g and 900 g, a 5 g scoop, and 65 specific lot numbers (410274 to 416903). The label image the certifier keeps on file reads 'Creatine Monohydrate 5 g', 90 servings, 'manufactured in the USA using US & imported ingredients for: Thorne Research, Inc.' The label on the brand's product page today carries the same ingredient, scoop and maker lines, says 'About 90' servings, adds a street address, and shows the NSF Certified for Sport seal; the tub's front reads 'Net Wt 16 oz (450 g)'. The brand sells the same product in 90- and 180-serving tubs, and 180 servings of 5 g is the listing's 900 g. We did not test the product, and nothing here is a measurement of what is in the tub.

  • our own desk check — nsfsport.com

    Read on 2026-09-23 from the same public product search. Its brand menu offers both 'Thorne' and 'Thorne®'. Searching 'Thorne' returned 21 listings, including the one cited above. Searching 'Thorne®' returned 45 different listings, among them two named 'Thorne® Creatine': an unflavoured powder at 453 g with 7 named lots (listing 1204244), and 30 single-serve stick packs (listing 1663109). The label the certifier keeps for the 453 g listing reads 'manufactured in the USA using US & imported ingredients by: Thorne Research, Inc.', where the current label and the listing above both read 'for:'. Neither search returns the other's listings.
