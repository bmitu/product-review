Caraway Ceramic Cookware Set — Partly measured — never tested in use

Caraway says: “Thoughtfully crafted without PTFE, PFOA, and PFAS, our proprietary non-stick ceramic offers effortless cooking, easy cleanup, and peace of mind at every meal.”

their own page · read 2026-09-23

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable only by a new independent measurement

Bottom line. Caraway publishes lab results for its ceramic coating: no PFAS detected in the samples it sent. Nobody has measured what a pan sheds after years of real cooking, and no lab sells that test off the shelf.

What would settle it: Food-simulant migration testing on pans put through an accelerated service life — 200 heat cycles to 230 °C, 50 dishwasher cycles and standardised abrasion — assaying total organic fluorine and the 20+ heavy metals in the simulant, against unused controls from the same lot.
Estimated cost: not yet priced

Evidence

  • measurement — mixed, funded by Caraway

    https://app.lightlabs.com/comprehensive-testing-report/154/938

    Fetched Caraway's Light Labs report on its ceramic coating: PFAS not detected in submitted samples, with no statement of the samples' age or use.

    This is real testing and it answers half of the claim. ‘Without PTFE, PFOA, and PFAS’ is a statement about what the coating is made of, and on the samples Caraway sent, the lab found none of the 660 PFAS it looked for. ‘Peace of mind at every meal’ is a statement about years of use, and the report says in its own words that it does not reach that far: it describes a snapshot of submitted samples and disclaims future stability. The closest thing to a use test in it is a one-time soak in food simulants, which is not a pan that has been cooked on.

“At every meal” is a claim about a pan's whole service life, and no test of an unused pan reaches it. That is the gap every ceramic-coating argument actually turns on, and it is the one nobody has funded — every cookware dataset we have found so far was paid for by the brand selling the pan, Caraway's own included. It is also the measurement where a brand-funded result would be least believable, which is precisely why it stays unmade.

WHAT IT WOULD COST, and why there is no single price. This is not a catalogue test. It is an accelerated-ageing step and a migration step at two different laboratories with an untested handoff between them, so no lab quotes it as one job. The figures below are assembled from published component prices — a build-up, not a quote, and we will not print it as though it were one.

Build-up for 6 units (3 aged + 3 unused controls from one lot), one pan model. FX as of 2026-09-18: EUR/USD 1.1483, HKD/USD 0.127465. • Accelerated service life — SGS publishes EN 12983-1 at HK$4,700 and EN 12983-2 (ceramic cookware) at HK$3,700, 9–13 days with coating. HK$8,400 ≈ $1,071. This is a PROXY: EN 12983 is a standardised compliance battery, not the 200-heat-cycle / 50-dishwasher / abrasion protocol described above. Overlapping, not equivalent. • Migration — Measurlabs publishes ‘Migration of heavy metals from ceramics, glass, and enameled articles’ at €190/sample. 6 × €190 = €1,140 ≈ $1,309. (Its ‘specific release of elements from metals and alloys intended for repeated use’ at €771/sample may fit an aluminium-cored pan better, and would add ~$5,300 for six units.) • Total organic fluorine in the simulant — Measurlabs publishes €368–630 per sample plus a €97 per-order fee. 6 samples = €2,305–3,877 ≈ $2,647–$4,452. • Indicative total: ~$5,000–$6,800, excluding the pans, shipping, simulant preparation and any re-test.

One cheaper number that does NOT apply, recorded so nobody budgets against it: the University of Arizona ALEC core facility lists total organic fluorine at $82.00/sample, about a fifth of the commercial rate — but that is internal pricing requiring a University of Arizona account, and the facility says costs differ for outside users. An academic partnership that made it reachable would change this test's economics more than any vendor negotiation. That is a route worth chasing, not a price you can pay today.

How we checked

  • measurement — app.lightlabs.com

    Retrieved 2026-09-23 from the lab's own domain, embedded on carawayhome.com/pages/third-party-testing. 660 PFAS and total organic fluorine reported ‘Not detected’ with no detection limit stated; 25 heavy metals below stated limits; 6 one-time extraction tests into food simulants below stated limits; tested 22 January to 2 March 2026. The lab's own disclaimer: ‘Results reflect laboratory analysis of submitted samples at the time of testing… Results do not guarantee future performance, stability, or shelf life.’ Nothing in the report describes a sample as used, heated or aged.
