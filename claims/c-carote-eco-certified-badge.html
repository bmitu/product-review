Carote every cookware set in Carote's US web store (badge on all ten product pages) — Cannot be checked as worded

Carote says: “Eco-certified”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

NOT CHECKABLE AS WORDED — no evidence could settle this either way · unfalsifiable as worded

Bottom line. Carote shows 'Eco-certified' on every set it sells, with no certifier, standard or benefit named. There is nothing to check it against.

A certification can only be checked against the certifier's own records, and this badge names no certifier, no standard and no environmental benefit. There is no register to look it up in and nothing specific to test. The same two words appear on every set Carote sells, from ceramic nonstick to uncoated stainless, so they do not describe anything about a particular pan either.

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.mycarote.com/products/cashew-detachable-cookware-set-19pcs

    All ten product pages in Carote's US store show 'Eco-certified' twice, beside other badges, with no certifier, logo or link.

    The badge sits in the same row as 'Modern' and 'Warranty', and is repeated on stainless and ceramic sets alike. It reads as part of the store's page design rather than something awarded to one product.

  • document — mixed, independent

    https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260/section-260.6

    The FTC's Green Guides, read from the official eCFR: a seal that does not say what it certifies likely implies a broad environmental benefit.

    This is the US regulator's guidance on exactly this kind of label. It does not rule on Carote, and we are not saying Carote broke it. It explains why a certification with no basis stated gives a shopper nothing to go on.

'Certified' promises that someone other than the brand checked something. This badge does not say who, or what. We looked on the product pages, across Carote's site and in a web search, and found no certificate or certifier behind it.

It also appears on every set in the store, the uncoated stainless ones included, in the same row as 'Modern' and 'Warranty'. That makes it look like a feature of the store's design rather than an award to a product. The FTC's guidance on green marketing says a seal that does not state its basis tends to suggest a broad environmental benefit, and that brands should not use one. A shopper should give this badge no weight either way.

How we checked

  • our own desk check — mycarote.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in the HTML the server sends. On the Cashew set page the words appear in two badge lists: 'PFOA Free, PFOS Free, Eco-certified' under the title, and 'Modern, Eco-certified, Warranty' further down. Each is plain text beside a generic tick icon, with no logo, certifier name or link. We fetched every product in the store's catalogue (ten sets, including the Flex and Tri-Ply stainless sets and the Sakura ceramic set); each page carries the phrase exactly twice. Our sweep of Carote's site (205 addresses in its sitemap, 11 pages fetched) found no certificate, test report or named certifier. Re-checked on 30 September: the sweep had missed two unlinked Carote pages, a PTFE notice naming no product and an empty chemicals-disclosure page (see c-carote-cashew-without-pfoa-pfos); neither mentions a certification.

  • document — ecfr.gov

    Read on 2026-09-27 from the eCFR's rendering service for 16 CFR 260.6, 'Certifications and seals of approval'. Paragraph (d): 'A marketer's use of an environmental certification or seal of approval likely conveys that the product offers a general environmental benefit (see § 260.4) if the certification or seal does not convey the basis for the certification or seal, either through the name or some other means. Because it is highly unlikely that marketers can substantiate general environmental benefit claims, marketers should not use environmental certifications or seals that do not convey the basis for the certification.' Section 260.4(b) says 'marketers should not make unqualified general environmental benefit claims.'
