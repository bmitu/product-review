Le Creuset Essential Non-stick Ceramic Fry Pan, 10-inch — Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence

Le Creuset says: “All-over ceramic coating is made without PFAS”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Le Creuset says its Essential ceramic coating is made without PFAS. Its own filing for the pan lists nothing fluorinated, while its conventional nonstick pan's filing names PTFE. That holds, on the brand's paperwork.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by Le Creuset

    https://app.salsify.com/catalogs/998c54a9-7db5-4124-9dd8-8a7a6047a6c5/products/51314260010098

    Le Creuset's own California chemical disclosure for this exact pan (10-inch, SKU 51314026001001), read in full.

    California's cookware law makes a maker list certain chemicals intentionally added to a pan's handle or cooking surface, and PFAS are on that list. Le Creuset's filing for this pan names seven substances and no PFAS. The metals it lists are the ones in stainless steel, which this pan has in its handle, base and rim. The filing does not name the coating's ingredients as such, and we have not tried to work out which listed substance comes from which part. There is no Colorado entry, which is what a pan with no PFAS to report would be expected to show, but the page does not say so.

  • document — supports, funded by Le Creuset

    https://app.salsify.com/catalogs/998c54a9-7db5-4124-9dd8-8a7a6047a6c5/products/51112260012100

    For comparison, Le Creuset's disclosure for its Toughened Non-stick PRO 10-inch pan, read in full: it has a PFAS section naming PTFE, FEP and PFA.

    This is what makes the ceramic pan's filing worth more than its silence. Le Creuset does declare PFAS where it uses them: its conventional nonstick pan carries a PFAS section on the same site and on its own product page. The ceramic pan, filed the same way, has none. A brand that labels fluoropolymers where they are, and not where they aren't, is telling a consistent story.

  • our own desk check — supports, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.lecreuset.com/essential-nonstick-ceramic-fry-pan-10-in/51314026001001.html

    The 10-inch pan's product page read in full: the claim is live, the coating is unnamed, and no test report is linked.

    The claim is narrow and specific: the coating, all over the pan, is made without PFAS. That is the kind of claim a disclosure filing can check, because it is about what goes into the pan. The page names no coating brand or chemistry beyond 'ceramic'.

This checks out on the brand's own paperwork. Le Creuset files a chemical disclosure for each pan, as California requires, and the filing for this one lists seven substances with no PFAS among them. That is the same kind of support we accepted for Lodge's cast iron and Misen's carbon steel. And the filing's silence means something here: Le Creuset's conventional nonstick pan, filed on the same site, carries a separate section naming PTFE, FEP and PFA. The ceramic pan has no such section.

Three limits. The disclosure describes what goes into the pan; nobody has published a test of what is in the finished coating. The filing lists stainless-steel metals without saying which part of the pan each comes from, and it does not name the coating's own ingredients. And Le Creuset's site blocks automated reading, so we read these pages in an ordinary browser and could not sweep the whole site for a test report the way we do for other brands. A web search found none.

The contrast with Made In is worth drawing. Made In's ceramic pan makes the same kind of claim, but Made In's disclosure does not mention that pan at all. Le Creuset's covers this exact pan by SKU.
