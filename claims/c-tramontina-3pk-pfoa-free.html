Tramontina 3 Pk Fry Pans with Nonstick Interior (SKU 80124/043DS) — Nobody has measured this

Tramontina says: “You can rest easy knowing these fry pans are PFOA free.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable only by a new independent measurement

Bottom line. Tramontina says these nonstick pans are PFOA free, and publishes no test. Its own disclosure, linked from the same page, says its cookware has PFAS added for nonstick properties, without saying which products.

What would settle it: A lab analysis of the nonstick interior from new production pans: a targeted PFAS panel that includes PFOA, reported with its detection limit, plus a total-fluorine measurement of the coating, which would show whether the 'premium nonstick interior finish' is a fluoropolymer at all.
Estimated cost: not yet priced

Evidence

  • document — mixed, funded by Tramontina USA

    https://tramontina.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/12403339967763-Chemicals-Disclosure

    Tramontina's chemicals disclosure, linked from every product page, read in full: its cookware has intentionally added PFAS.

    This is the whole disclosure. It confirms that Tramontina adds PFAS to cookware for nonstick properties, but it does not say which products or which PFAS, so it cannot tell a reader what is in this particular pan. It says nothing about PFOA.

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.tramontina.com/products/3-pk-fry-pans-with-nonstick-interior

    The product page read in full: 'PFOA free', a 'Premium nonstick interior finish', and no word on what that finish is.

    The product page names the one chemical it can say is absent and is silent on what the nonstick interior is actually made of.

'PFOA free' can only be checked by testing the pan, and Tramontina publishes no test, so we cannot say whether it is true. What we can say is how little it tells a shopper. The product page does not say what the 'premium nonstick interior finish' is. The chemicals disclosure linked from its footer says Tramontina adds PFAS to its cookware to make it nonstick, but it names no products and no chemicals.

So a shopper who reads 'PFOA free' as 'free of forever chemicals' is being given the narrowest possible answer to a broader question. The disclosure does not say this particular pan contains PFAS, and we are not saying so either. The page could settle it in one line, and does not.

Neither PTFE nor PFAS appears on the page. Our sweep of Tramontina's site (1,663 addresses in its sitemap, 31 pages fetched) found no test report; the only laboratory the site names is NSF, in blog posts about NSF food-equipment certification, which is not a PFOA test.
