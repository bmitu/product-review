Misen Carbon Nonstick pans — Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence

Misen says: “No, Carbon Nonstick™ is free of PFAS, PTFE, and PFOA.”

their own page · read 2026-09-26

SUPPORTED — evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Misen says Carbon Nonstick is free of PFAS. Its own state disclosures list none for carbon steel while naming PTFE for the nonstick it discontinued, and the pan is uncoated. No one has published a test of it.

Evidence

  • document — supports, funded by Misen

    https://misen.com/pages/disclosures

    Misen's California and Colorado chemical disclosure page, read in full: every material table and the note on its discontinued nonstick.

    This is the brand's own disclosure, filed under two state laws that require cookware makers to name certain chemicals intentionally added to the cooking surface or handle. It is useful for more than its silence. On the same page Misen does name PTFE, FEP and PFA, for the traditional nonstick line it stopped selling at the end of 2024. A brand that lists fluoropolymers where it used them, and lists none for its carbon steel, is telling a consistent story.

  • our own desk check — supports, we are not a neutral party here

    https://misen.com/products/carbon-nonstick-pan-deeper-10-inch

    The Carbon Nonstick product page read in full, including its FAQ, for what the pan is and how it is made nonstick.

    The pan's nonstick behaviour, as Misen describes it, comes from nitriding the steel and from seasoning that builds up with cooking oil, not from a coating. Nothing in that account needs a fluorinated chemical, and nothing on the page or in the disclosure mentions one.

A claim that checks out as far as reading can take it.

The pan is nitrided carbon steel with no coating, and Misen's legally required chemical disclosure lists no fluorinated chemicals for its carbon steel. What makes that disclosure worth trusting is the table next to it: for the PTFE nonstick line Misen discontinued in 2024, the same page lists PTFE, FEP and PFA plainly. This is a brand that has disclosed PFAS when its products contained them.

The limit is worth stating. Those disclosure laws cover chemicals a maker intentionally adds. 'Free of PFAS' is a slightly stronger claim, and the only way to confirm it outright is to test the pan, which neither Misen nor anyone else appears to have published. For an uncoated steel pan there is no obvious way for PFAS to get there. That is a reason to believe the claim, not a measurement confirming it.

How we checked

  • document — misen.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 in the HTML the server sends. The page carries separate tables for enameled cast iron, stainless steel cookware and bakeware, traditional nonstick (discontinued as of 31 December 2024), carbon steel, prep tools, knives and spoons, and more. The carbon steel table lists iron, manganese and manganese compounds, aluminum, and phosphorus. The traditional nonstick table, and a paragraph above the tables, list PTFE, FEP and PFA.

  • our own desk check — misen.com

    Read on 2026-09-26 in the HTML the server sends. The claim is the answer to the FAQ question 'Does Carbon Nonstick™ contain PFAS, PTFE, or PFOA?'. The page describes the pan as 'Nitrided carbon steel for a coating-free, naturally nonstick cooking surface' with an aluminum core, and says it is not pre-seasoned. We swept Misen's site for a published test report (838 addresses in its sitemap, 32 pages fetched) and found none.
