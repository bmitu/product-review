Le Creuset all Le Creuset cookware (Chemical Disclosures page) — Nothing published that a reader could check

Le Creuset says: “Le Creuset is committed to product quality and safety, regularly conducting internal and reputable third-party laboratory evaluations. For over 100 years, our products have been consistently safe to use and comply with all federal safety regulations established by the Food & Drug Administration.”

their own page · read 2026-09-27

UNSUPPORTED — we went and checked; here is what we found · checkable by reading a document

Bottom line. Le Creuset says reputable third-party labs evaluate its cookware and that it meets FDA rules. It names no lab and links no result, so a reader cannot check either.

Evidence

  • our own desk check — mixed, we are not a neutral party here

    https://www.lecreuset.com/chemical-disclosures-for-cookware.html

    Le Creuset's Chemical Disclosures page read in full; it names no laboratory, links no report, and links only the disclosure search and California's database.

    The sentence tells readers that outside laboratories evaluate the cookware and gives them nothing to look at: no lab, no report, no test and no product. That is not evidence the testing did not happen. It means a reader has only Le Creuset's word that it did. The page does link real documents, but they are the state-required chemical disclosures, which list ingredients and are not lab results.

  • document — mixed, funded by Le Creuset

    https://app.salsify.com/catalogs/998c54a9-7db5-4124-9dd8-8a7a6047a6c5/products/20182260900422

    Le Creuset's disclosures for the 10.25-inch Signature Skillet in four colours: red and orange list cadmium pigments, but not on the cooking surface.

    This is more than most brands tell you. Le Creuset's filings say which colours use cadmium pigments and, by leaving them off the food-contact list, that the cadmium is in the outside enamel, not the black cooking surface. That is a clearer answer than Lodge's chart gives for its red and orange pieces. It still does not show a test: a disclosure lists what is added on purpose, and the question the third-party testing claim invites, how much lead or cadmium can come out, is one only a published leach result can answer.

  • measurement — mixed, independent

    https://tamararubin.com/2021/10/vintage-green-and-white-enamel-le-creuset-cooking-pot-positive-for-varying-levels-of-lead-and-cadmium

    Posted 2021: handheld X-ray fluorescence readings of one vintage Le Creuset pot of unstated age: lead and cadmium in both enamels.

    This is the only independent measurement of a Le Creuset piece we found, and it says little about what Le Creuset sells today. It is one vintage pot of unknown age. XRF measures what is in the enamel, not how much leaches into food, and food-safety limits for enamel are built on leaching. What it does show is why a published leach test would mean more than a sentence saying labs have looked: lead and cadmium have been found in Le Creuset enamel, including a trace on the cooking surface of this old pot.

Le Creuset puts this sentence at the top of its Chemical Disclosures page, where it reads as if the page will back it up. The documents the page links are the chemical disclosures California requires: lists of ingredients by product, not laboratory results. There is no lab named, no test, no product and no result.

That is the same position as GreenPan's and Lodge's third-party testing claims, and it deserves the same careful reading. It is not evidence the testing does not happen. It means a shopper who wants to see it has nowhere to go. The second half, compliance with FDA rules, is the kind of statement a leach test for lead and cadmium would back. Le Creuset has not published one.

Le Creuset's own filings do answer one question well. For its Signature skillet, the red and orange colours list cadmium pigments for the pan, and the food-contact list, the black enamel you cook on, does not include them. So the cadmium is on the outside.

The one independent measurement we found is a consumer advocate's handheld reading of a single vintage pot of unknown age, which found lead and cadmium in its enamel, mostly on the outside. It does not describe a pot sold today, and it measures what is in the enamel rather than what leaches out. It is a reason the missing results would be worth publishing, not a finding against current products.

One limit on our side: Le Creuset's site blocks automated reading, so we read its pages in an ordinary browser and could not sweep the whole site for a report. A web search found none.

How we checked

  • our own desk check — lecreuset.com

    Le Creuset's site turns away automated requests, so these pages were opened in an ordinary browser on 2026-09-27 and saved whole; we read the saved copies. The same wall meant we could not sweep the rest of the site for a published test report the way we do for other brands. The claim is live on the page word for word. The page names no laboratory, test method, product or result. Its only links are to the per-product disclosure search (hosted on Salsify) and to California's chemical database, calsafer.dtsc.ca.gov. A web search on 2026-09-27 for a published Le Creuset laboratory report on lead, cadmium or PFAS found none published by Le Creuset.

  • document — app.salsify.com

    Read on 2026-09-27 in an ordinary browser, from Le Creuset's Salsify-hosted disclosure search. Enameled Cast Iron Signature Round Skillet, 10.25 in., black interior. Licorice (20182260000422) and Azure (20182262200422): silica dust (quartz), boric acid and disodium tetraborate, for the whole pan and for the food-contact surface. Flame (20182260900422) adds, for the whole pan only, 'Pigment Cd Sulphoselenide Red', 'Pigment Cd Sulphoselenide Orange' and 'Pigment Cd Zinc Sulphide Yellow'. Cerise (20182260600422) adds the red and orange cadmium pigments, again for the whole pan only. The food-contact list for every colour is the same three substances, with no cadmium and no lead.

  • measurement — tamararubin.com

    Read on 2026-09-27. The post, dated 1 October 2021 and described by its author as a placeholder carrying only the XRF data, reports two 60-second readings of a 'vintage' green and white enamel Le Creuset pot. White interior (food surface): lead 24 ± 8 ppm, cadmium 38 ± 5 ppm. Green exterior (bottom): lead 35 ± 9 ppm, cadmium 2,815 ± 68 ppm. The post gives no year of manufacture.
