Nonstick, coating not named

What a nonstick, coating not named cooking surface is and what is known about it, one sourced sentence at a time; then every product line we list with this surface, and the claims we have checked about them. We do not say which to buy.

9 product lines from 4 brands. 3 checked claims. See every brand.

How we decide a line's surface: The cooking surfaces of the frying pans and skillets the brand sells, in the brand's own words. We say PTFE or ceramic only where the brand's own page does (Teflon, a trademark for PTFE coatings, counts as naming PTFE); otherwise the coating is 'not named'.

What it is and what is known

  1. When the Ecology Center tested 14 nonstick cooking pans in 2020 to identify their coatings, 11 were PTFE, two were silicon dioxide ('ceramic') and one was a BPA-based epoxy.
    Source: Ecology Center (Healthy Stuff Lab), a Michigan nonprofit, “What’s Cooking? PFAS and Other Chemical Hazards in Nonstick Cooking and Baking Pans” (nonprofit research lab), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “Our testing found PTFE coatings on 11 of the 14 nonstick cooking pans. Of the remaining three pans, two were coated with silicon dioxide (“ceramic”) and one with BPA-based epoxy” ('Cooking Pans' section; report published December 8, 2020)
  2. In the same tests, several pans listed only a trade name for their coating (QuanTanium, Granitestone, DuPont Autograph, Greblon); the lab identified all four as PTFE.
    Source: Ecology Center (Healthy Stuff Lab), a Michigan nonprofit, “What’s Cooking? PFAS and Other Chemical Hazards in Nonstick Cooking and Baking Pans” (nonprofit research lab), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “Several cooking pans listed trade names for their coatings, which our testing identified as PTFE: QuanTanium, Granitestone, DuPont Autograph, and Greblon.” (Section on how consumers can know if a pan contains PFAS)
  3. The Ecology Center also found that most pans labeled PFOA-free were coated with PTFE without clearly saying so.
    Source: Ecology Center (Healthy Stuff Lab), a Michigan nonprofit, “What’s Cooking? PFAS and Other Chemical Hazards in Nonstick Cooking and Baking Pans” (nonprofit research lab), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “In fact, most pans labeled PFOA-free were coated with PTFE without clearly indicating that.” (Under the heading 'PFOA-free doesn’t mean PFAS-free')
  4. A coating's trade name need not say what it is: T-fal's own product page calls its 'Platinum' nonstick its longest-lasting PTFE nonstick.
    Source: T-fal, “Professional VX3 Platinum Non-Stick 12 inch Frypan” (the brand's own words, not independent), read 2026-09-30.
    We list T-fal's Professional VX3 line under PTFE nonstick, not here.
    Their words
    “Platinum Non-Stick coating: Our longest lasting PTFE non-stick”
  5. T-fal describes the coating on its Ultimate Hard Anodized frypans as a 'titanium non-stick coating'.
    Source: T-fal, “Ultimate Hard Anodized Non-Stick Cookware 2 Piece Frypan Set” (the brand's own words, not independent), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “Titanium non-stick coating keeps food sliding smoothly”
  6. The same T-fal page says the product contains PTFE for its nonstick properties. On this pan, 'titanium' is the name of a PTFE coating, not a titanium cooking surface.
    Source: T-fal, “Ultimate Hard Anodized Non-Stick Cookware 2 Piece Frypan Set” (the brand's own words, not independent), read 2026-09-30.
    We list T-fal's Ultimate Hard Anodized line under PTFE nonstick, not here.
    Their words
    “This product contains PTFE for its nonstick properties.”
  7. Since 1 January 2024, California law has required cookware makers to list, on the product label, intentionally added chemicals from a state-designated list that are in the handle or any food-contact surface, introduced by the words 'This product contains:'.
    Source: California Legislature, “California Health and Safety Code § 109011” (the law's text), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “Commencing on January 1, 2024, a manufacturer of cookware sold in the state that contains one or more intentionally added chemicals present on the designated list in the handle of the product or in any product surface that comes into contact with food, foodstuffs, or beverages shall list the presence of those chemicals on the product label. The list of intentionally added chemicals shall be introduced by the phrase “This product contains:”” (Subdivision (a). Subdivision (b) extends the statement to 'the product listing for online sales'.)
