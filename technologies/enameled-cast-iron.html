What it is and what is known

  1. The Porcelain Enamel Institute, the North American enamel industry's trade group, describes porcelain enamel as a form of glass bonded to metal at high temperature.
    Source: Porcelain Enamel Institute, “Consumer Information” (industry group, not independent), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “porcelain enamel is a form of glass bonded to metal on a molecular level at high temperature.”
  2. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration says no FDA regulation authorizes lead as a component of cookware or of any other surface that touches food. This is a rule for all cookware, not for enamel in particular.
    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, “Lead in Food and Foodwares” (regulator), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “There are no FDA regulations that authorize lead for use as a color additive or food additive (including as a component of foodware, cookware, or food contact surfaces).”
  3. The FDA's compliance guide that sets numeric limits on how much lead a dish or pot may release defines its categories for ceramic articles.
    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, “CPG Sec. 545.450 Pottery (Ceramics); Import and Domestic - Lead Contamination” (regulator), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “The categories of ceramic articles used in the preparation, serving or storage of food, are defined as follows:” (Regulatory action guidance, table of criteria)
  4. Enamelled cookware can be tested for lead and cadmium the same way as glazed pottery: in a 1983 study, 14 laboratories validated a hot-acid leaching method, published by the World Health Organization, for both ceramic and enamelled ware.
    Source: Journal of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (abstract served by the U.S. National Library of Medicine), “Hot leaching of ceramic and enameled cookware: collaborative study (J Assoc Off Anal Chem 1983;66(3):610-9), abstract via PubMed” (peer-reviewed study), read 2026-09-30.
    Their words
    “A hot leach method published by the World Health Organization for determining Pb and Cd in ceramic and enameled ware was collaboratively studied in 14 laboratories.”
