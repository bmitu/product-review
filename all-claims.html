All claims

Brand claims, checked against the documents that decide them.

Each page below takes one thing a brand says about its product and checks it against the document that could settle it: a regulator's register, a certifier's listing, a lab report, a filed label. Every verdict says who produced the evidence and who paid for it, and links to it so you can check our reading yourself.

Pieces

  • PFAS in cookware: what the brands claim, and what they can show

    Every cookware brand in this set makes a PFAS claim on its own page. The claims are not the interesting part — who paid for the evidence behind them is. Here is each claim, its source, and the funder of every document backing it.

  • Prebiotic soda: the number on the can, and the number the claim needs

    Two numbers decide whether a prebiotic soda does what its label implies, and only one of them is printed on the can. Here is the arithmetic, the published threshold it runs against, and the single cheap lab test that would close the gap. No lab was needed to get this far — only the label and one regulator's published condition of use.

  • Two brands, the same check, opposite answers

    Both of these claims were checked the same way, for nothing. A company points at a public list kept by a regulator. The regulator publishes that list. You download it and you count.

  • Claims nobody has measured

    These are claims where no evidence exists in either direction — not disputed, just unmeasured. Each one is a test we could run if enough readers want it. Where a price is shown, it is built from a laboratory's own published schedule and named in the record; where it says not yet priced, no lab publishes a price for that workflow and we will not guess one — unpriced means unquoted, not cheap.

  • "We publish the reports" — so we went to read them

    A brand that tests its cookware and publishes the results is already doing more than most: every cookware dataset we have found so far was paid for by the brand selling the pan, and this one says out loud that it will show its work. So we took the claim at face value and went to fetch the reports, following the instruction on the brand's own page. Below are three claims from that page and the one behind it — one we could check by going to read it, one that no document can settle and only a new measurement could, and one that no measurement can settle at all. What each of those means is said in the row.

cookware

  • All-Clad Ceramic Nonstick cookware (NS Pro and HA1 Ceramic Nonstick) — “Each Ceramic Nonstick piece is backed by All-Clad’s limited lifetime warranty, reflecting our commitment to quality materials, thoughtful design, and cookware you can trust in your kitchen.”

    Does not hold up — on the seller's own documents · checked 2026-09-26

    All-Clad's warranty page says its ceramic nonstick has a lifetime warranty. The warranty document linked beneath that sentence says one year, and it excludes coating wear. The one-year figure on the product page is the right one.

  • Caraway — “We don't just claim to be PFAS-free. We prove it—and we publish the reports.”

    Holds up — but only on the seller's own evidence · checked 2026-09-30

    Caraway does publish its reports: the page it names embeds a lab report on its ceramic coating, with every PFAS result not detected. Caraway paid for it, and none of the PFAS results says how low the test could see.

  • Caraway Ceramic Cookware Set — “Non-Toxic Coating”

    Cannot be checked as worded · checked 2026-09-20

    “Non-toxic” is not a claim any test can check. The specific absences Caraway lists underneath it are, and each one can be checked on its own.

  • Caraway Ceramic Cookware Set — “Thoughtfully crafted without PTFE, PFOA, and PFAS, our proprietary non-stick ceramic offers effortless cooking, easy cleanup, and peace of mind at every meal.”

    Partly measured — never tested in use · checked 2026-09-23

    Caraway publishes lab results for its ceramic coating: no PFAS detected in the samples it sent. Nobody has measured what a pan sheds after years of real cooking, and no lab sells that test off the shelf.

  • Caraway Cookware, bakeware, kettle, prep set, glass lids, kitchen gadgets and cast iron (California disclosure) — “The following items are listed to maintain compliance with California Bill AB-1200 and are compiled from the CalSafer Candidate Chemical list.”

    Holds up · checked 2026-09-27

    Caraway publishes the chemical disclosure California requires. Across six authoritative lists read in full it is right 68 times out of 72; all four misses sit on its cadmium row, and we cannot say whether the state or the company made them.

  • Carote Cashew Detachable Cookware Set, 19 pieces — “Cook with confidence using Carote’s nonstick cookware, made without PFOA, PFOS, or other harmful chemicals—designed for safer, easier everyday meals.”

    Cannot be checked as worded · checked 2026-09-30

    Carote says the Cashew set is made without PFOA, PFOS 'or other harmful chemicals'. It never says what the coating is, and never mentions PTFE. An unlinked Carote page says some product of theirs 'contains PTFE' without naming which.

  • Carote every cookware set in Carote's US web store (badge on all ten product pages) — “Eco-certified”

    Carote shows 'Eco-certified' on every set it sells, with no certifier, standard or benefit named. There is nothing to check it against.

  • GreenPan — “Lastly, as part of our compliance with California’s AB1200 bill, any of the 3,000+ designated substances of concern that need to be disclosed are done so and listed openly on our website, GreenPan.us, meaning that, contrary to what some articles allege, GreenPan provides a high level of disclosure.”

    Cannot be checked as worded · checked 2026-09-20

  • GreenPan Thermolon ceramic nonstick coating — “We confirm that GreenPan’s™ Thermolon™ coated food contact surface is Free Of: … • And 247 Substances of Very High Concern (SVHC) that are listed under the European REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorization and Restriction of Chemicals) Program including the siloxanes of concern, namely D4, D5, D6 and Octamethyltrisiloxane”

    Does not hold up · checked 2026-09-20

    GreenPan says its coating is free of the 247 Substances of Very High Concern on Europe’s REACH list. That list held 247 entries for five months in 2025 and holds 253 now. The figure was right when written; the page never says when that was.

    Nobody has measured this · checked 2026-09-20

  • GreenPan Thermolon ceramic nonstick coating — “Our Thermolon™ coating and GreenPan™ cookware undergo independent testing by highly professional, accredited third-party labs to ensure compliance with our product claims, quality standards, and numerous safety regulations.”

    GreenPan says accredited third-party labs test its coating. Across all 212 pages of its site there is no report, no certificate number and no laboratory named — and unlike some rivals, it never promised to publish them.
