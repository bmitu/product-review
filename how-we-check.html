How we check

We start from the exact words a brand uses, copied from its own page with the date we read it. Then we ask one question: what document could settle this? Some claims point at one — a certifier's register, a government filing, a published lab report. Some can be settled by arithmetic over numbers already published. Some could only be settled by testing the product, and nobody has. And some are worded so that nothing could settle them.

Four kinds of claim

Type A
Checkable by reading a document.
Type B
Checkable by arithmetic over published numbers.
Type C
Checkable only by a new independent measurement.
Type D
Unfalsifiable as worded.

How far the evidence goes

A verdict says whether the claim holds up. A separate rating says how much weight that verdict can bear, and it depends on who produced the evidence. A brand's own report, a certifier the brand pays, or a label the brand files can support a claim, but it cannot put it in the top rating. Only an independent measurement of the product can.

Top tier
Independently measured.
Supported
Evidence exists; nobody independent has measured the product.
Unsupported
No qualifying evidence.

What each piece of evidence tells you

Every item of evidence is marked with its direction (supports, refutes, or mixed) and its funder. Items marked as our own desk check are what we found when we went to the document ourselves. We are not a neutral party to our own checking, so those items never raise a rating. Each record ends with a section on how we checked, including the commands that reproduce the result where there are any.

What we do not do

We have not tested any product on this site. Where a page says a claim holds up, it means the document behind the claim says what the brand says it does, not that we measured what is in the product. Products change, so every record carries the date it was last checked.
